# Development of a composite using calcined bone powder and silane cross-linked alginate as bone substitute material 

-> Calcined bone is an attractive natural material for use as a bone substitute because of its cost-effectiveness and high biocompatibility.
-> calcination process has significantly weakened the mechancial properties.

-> calcined bovine bone powder reinforced with silance cross-linked alginate was prepared to assess its biocompactibility, osteoconductivity and mechanical compactibility as bone substitute material.


-> culture studies with osteoblast-like cells showed no cytotoxicity toward the composite and exhibited general cell proliferative properties in its presence. 

-> the composite reduced the alkaline phosphatase activity of osteoblasts but led to significant noncellular apatite 

-> quasi-static compression tests of the composite revealed mechanical propertie comparable to thoose of human cancellous bone.

-> The mechanical properties remained stable under wet conditions and did not deteriorate significantly even after 2 weeks of immersion in simulated body fluid at 37*C

-> Results showed that this composite, composed of calcined bone powder and silane cross-linked alginate, is a promising bone substitute material with biocompatibility, 

# Introduction

-> initial rigidity and mechanical compatibiliity are essential elements to avoid stress shileding 

-> in this study a novel bone substitue matieral was developed by combining calcined bovin bone particle with alginate, a natural polysaccharide extracted from seaweek and a cost-effective 


-> calcined bone particle that contribute to mechanical rigidity and 
-> alginate fibers, which provide fracture resistance. 

-> this composite is expected to have high mechanical toughness and mechanical compatibility. 


-> alginate gel when placed in body fluids, as swelling of the gel occurs by leaching calcium ions from the gel.
-> consequently, the mechanical properties of the composite deteriorated considerably soon after transplantation.

-> covalent cross-linking of alginate fibers occurs through 3-amino-propyltriethoxysilane promoted silane coupling and alginate spontaneously form apatite by incorporationg calcium and phosphate ions from body fluids.

-> Therefore, it was hypothesized that the calcined bone powder/alginate composite could obtain the necessary mechanical rigidity, mechancial stability in body fluids, and osteoconductivity though silane coupling.

# Validation 
-> observing the changes in simulated body fluids (SBF)
-> cytotoxity of the composite using the cultured osteoblast cells was assessed and the impact of the composite on cell proliferation, differentiation and bone matix formation was investigated.


# Results 


# Mechanical properties of the composite material 

-> the maximum stress and elastic modulus of B/A-Si wer initially measured at 7.51+-2.20 MPa and 219+-66.9 MPa. before immersion
-> these properteis showed a decreasing trend with incrasing immersion time up to 2 weeks, 
-> no significant difference was found between observation days.

# Cytotoxicity
-> regardless of the perentage of extraction fluid used, the cell viability was approximately 95%, equivalent to when fresh medium was used for cultivation, and no statistically significant differences were observed between groups.

-> the composite had no effect on osteoblast proliferation. 

# observation
-> the mechanical properties of the composite were comparable to those of the human cancellous bone.
(good initial rigidity and adaptability)
-> these properties remained relatively stable even after 2 weeks of immersion in SBF and did not deteriorate significantly.
-> B/A-Si is not toxic to osteoblasts and has no noticeable effect on cell proliferation.
-> decrease in ALP activity.
-> incontrast, B/A-Si induced the formation of noncellular apatite. 
-> these findings suggest that this composite material has mechanical compatibility, biocompatibility and osteoconductive properties, making it a promising bone substitute material.
