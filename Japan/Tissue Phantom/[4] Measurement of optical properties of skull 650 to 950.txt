Measurement of the optical properties of the skull in the wavelength range 650-950 nm
(pig skull)

-> Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) is widely used to measure cerebral oxygenation and hemodynamics non-invasively. 
-> Light travelling to and from the brain must pass through the skull, which significantly affects photon propagation.

# Experimentally measure the skull's
-> Scattering phase function using goniometer
-> mean cosine g, (anisotropy factor)
-> Absorption coefficient (ua)
-> scattering coefficient (us)

# Values for g measured on six samples varied from 
-> 0.925+-0.014 nm at 650 nm
-> 0.945+-0.013 nm at 950 nm

# For measurement on 18 samples
# 650 nm
-> us=35+-0.7 mm-1
-> ua=0.04+-0.002 mm-1
# 950 nm
-> us=24+-0.6 mm-1
-> ua=0.05+-0.002 mm-1

# Characterize 

-> Direct phase-function measurements (goniometer)
-> Integrating-sphere reflectance/transmittance
-> Monte Carlo inversion with measured phase function.
->  PIG bones because it is similar to human bone
-> 60-90 micrometer for phase function measurement (sample size)
-> 2 mm absorption and scattering measurements (sample size)

# In order to quantify spectroscopy data and hence determine chromophore concentraitions, it is necessary to know the path length travelled by the light throug the tissues.
