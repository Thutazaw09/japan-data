# Double-integrating-sphere system for measuring the optical properties of tissue

# Abstract 
-> A system is described and evaluated for the simultaneous measurement of the intrinsic optical properties of tissue
# the scattering coefficient
# the absorption coefficient
# the anisotropy factor

# synthesizes the theory of two integrating spheres
-> an interventing scattering sample with the inverse adding-doubling algorithm, which employs the adding-doubling solution of the radiative transfer equation to determine the optical properties from the measurement of the ligh flux within each sphere and of the unscattered transmission.
-> allows for measurements to be made while the sample undergoes
-> heating
-> chemical change
-> or other external stimulus

an experimental validation of the system with tissue phantoms resulted in the determination of the optical properties with <5% deviation when the optical density was between 1 and 10 and the albedo was between 0.4 and 0.95

# Integrating sphere theory

-> Sphere formulas for both diffuse and collimated incident light are given since they are incessary for understanding the procedures for calibrating the spheres. 

A Single-sphere theory
-> to measure the reflected and transmitted light from a sample, we situated it as, 
the exit or entrance port of a sphere. 
-> Normally the detector is situated on the inner sphere surface. 
-> often tissue is placed between glass plates to prevent dehydration to provide mechanical support, and to minimize surface effects. the influence of these plates on the reflected and transmitted light may be calculated.
-> the theory requires that no direactly reflected light from the sample irradiates the detector, therefore a baffle is placed between the sample and the detector.
-> the integrating sphere theory accounts for the losses caused by absorption from the sphere wall and the light escaping the sphere. 
-> the power detected depends on the total light remaining within the sphere and on the relative (to the total sphere area) size of the detector.
